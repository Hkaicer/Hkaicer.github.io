Proactive Wireless Monitoring for Zero-Downtime Venues

24/7 monitoring for 1000+ access points across five-star hotels and international events, so outages get caught and fixed before anyone complains.

deployed in operations hardened remote access sanitized · no internal details

01 · context & problem

When Wi-Fi is contractual, not a convenience

BBRS runs the wireless infrastructure for some of Morocco's top five-star hotels and for international events such as GITEX Africa. For a guest closing a deal or running a live session over the network, an outage is a lost opportunity, not a minor annoyance. Availability is part of the contract.

The old model was reactive: a client noticed the problem, complained, and only then did support react. That is slow, and it hurts the experience of our clients' own guests.

my role

I designed and built the monitoring and automation: secure polling of the controllers, the health logic, the alerting flow, and the dashboards support teams rely on.

02 · architecture

From silent failures to instant alerts

Hover or tap a stage to see its role, or run the full cycle.

  • status check
  • AP down
  • alert + report
  • on-site fix
  • Polling of the wireless controllers over non-default ports, with access restricted by IP filtering.
  • Automated health logic checks every access point on a short cycle.
  • The moment a point goes down, an alert and a down-AP report reach the support team by email.
  • Grafana dashboards give 24/7 visibility of the whole fleet across every client.
  • Provisioning automated with Ansible, so new sites come online consistently.

03 · impact

Fixed before the complaint

Detection went from customer-complaint speed to near-instant, which cut both time-to-detect and time-to-repair. Support acts on a precise report instead of a vague guest message.

The remote-access work was driven by evidence. The logs showed heavy automated brute-force guessing user and password combinations from many countries. Moving administration off default ports and restricting access by IP filtering removed most of that noise. To be precise about it: the port change alone is not a security control, it just cuts commodity scanning. The IP restriction is what actually limits who can reach the service.

what it changed
  • Near-instant detection instead of complaint-driven.
  • Faster repair, with a precise down-AP report in hand.
  • A better experience for our clients and their guests.
  • Remote administration reachable from far fewer places, and much less automated noise.
